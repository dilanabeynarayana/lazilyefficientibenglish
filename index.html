Every IB Response Does Three Things

1. Add Implications

Students don't just name a theme. They break it into stages: what kind, why it matters, what the writer is claiming. The Stages of IDEAs and DICE/PACT give them that structure.

2. Add Connotations

Not "vivid imagery." Not "creates interest." Students deconstruct specific words, visuals, and elements to show what they connote — and link that to an idea. The 3‑Step Technique and Ways to Phrase Deconstructions make that automatic.

3. Add an Argument

A real thesis, not "this essay will explore." DDM — Deconstruct, Dichotomise, Merge — turns any question into a precise argument. The Stages of IDEAs then breaks that argument into paragraphs.

These Aren't Just Posters. They're a System.

Every resource on this site is designed to link to the others. Nothing is standalone. The Annotation Rubric feeds the TEEAL paragraph. The Stages of IDEAs feed the Elaboration, which feeds the feedback you give. In class, they become shorthand: "What stage is that idea?" "Is that a MUSE‑level deconstruction?" Over time, they spread across year levels and languages — a single, coherent system that students internalise.

These are lived documents. They're on the classroom wall, in the feedback cycle, and in the Google Docs students use every day. And if you're leading a department, the Eight Gates and the Analytical Continuum extend that coherence across your whole team.
